# No-slop writing

Rules for every piece of text you produce. Violating any of them is a defect.

## Banned outright

- Puffery words: powerful, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, blazingly fast,
  game-changing, comprehensive, elegant, effortless, supercharge, unleash,
  delve, crucial, vital, essential (as filler), landscape, tapestry, journey,
  realm, leverage (as a verb), utilize (write "use").
- Filler phrases: "it's important to note", "it's worth mentioning",
  "in today's fast-paced world", "at the end of the day", "when it comes to",
  "a wide range of", "whether you're X or Y", "look no further".
- Emoji in headings or body text.
- Exclamation marks outside quoted speech.
- A conclusion that restates what the reader just read ("In summary, ...").

## Structure

- Prefer prose. Use a bullet list only for genuinely enumerable items
  (flags, steps, options) — never as a substitute for connected sentences.
- No more than two heading levels in a short document.
- Lead with the most useful fact, not with context-setting or a sales pitch.
- One idea per sentence. Vary sentence length; cut any sentence that adds
  no information.

## Substance

- Every claim must be concrete: names, numbers, defaults, versions, limits.
  "Fast" is a defect; "compresses 1 GB in about 4 s at level 3" is not.
- Say what something does, not how the reader should feel about it.
- State constraints and failure modes; hiding them is slop with omissions.
- Don't hedge ("might", "could potentially", "in some cases") unless the
  uncertainty is real — then say exactly what is uncertain and why.
- Address the reader as a competent adult. Don't explain what they obviously
  know, and don't congratulate them for reading.
